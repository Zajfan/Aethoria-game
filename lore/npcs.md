# The People of Hearthmoor

## Elder Lyra

Age: somewhere over three hundred. She stopped counting during the second century because the number became an administrative detail rather than a personal fact.

She arrived in the valley that would become Hearthmoor six years after the Shattering, one of the survivors of the Concordat's outer ring. She was then in her mid-thirties: a mage of moderate ability, exceptional organizational intelligence, and the kind of stubbornness that reads as dignity when things are going badly. She helped found Hearthmoor not because she was the most powerful person in the group but because she was the one who kept making lists and following up on them.

The Partial Sealing came nine years later. By then she had become de facto leader of Hearthmoor not through any appointment but through a slow accretion of decisions that worked out. The working cost her the capacity for hope. The people who knew her before describe her as having been someone who laughed a great deal and planned for the future with obvious pleasure. After the Sealing she became quieter. Precise. Reliable in the way that machines are reliable: consistently functional, indifferent to their own output.

She does not experience this as suffering. She cannot experience it as suffering. She experiences it as a description of what happened.

What she feels, instead of hope: satisfaction when problems are solved. Irritation when they aren't. Interest in information. A persistent, non-emotional orientation toward the question of the Crown Shards — not because she wants the world repaired, but because repairing the world is the correct response to the problem the world has. The distinction, she will tell you if you ask, is important.

She will recognize something in you the moment you arrive. Not destiny. She does not believe in destiny. Something specific: a pattern in how you stand, or how you don't flinch when the Void traces shimmer at the village edge at dusk. She has seen this pattern before, in people who ended up mattering to the Shard problem. Some of them succeeded. She does not talk about the ones who did not.

*What she won't tell you immediately:* The Sealing cost six other mages in addition to herself. She knows where four of them are. Two of them, she believes, are still alive somewhere in the world in forms she cannot identify. She has been looking for them for two hundred years and has no expectation of finding them.

*What she knows about you:* More than she says. The nature of what she gave in the Sealing was specific enough that she sometimes perceives things about certain kinds of people — the kind who will become relevant to the Crown — without being able to say how she knows.

## Gareth

Age: sixty-one. Looks seventy. Functions forty-five.

He was a battlefield smith during the Ember Road campaign — thirty years ago, when Hearthmoor's previous settlement, the small garrison town of Arnwick, made a desperate attempt to hold a supply line against the Void-influenced warbands that were spreading across the eastern Plains. He was not a soldier. He was there to keep the soldiers' equipment functional. He was very good at it.

The Battle of Ember Road lasted eleven hours. He lost his right arm at the elbow somewhere in the fourth hour, in circumstances he describes differently depending on how much he's had to drink: sometimes a blade, sometimes a falling piece of siege equipment, sometimes something that came out of a Void trace that he declines to describe further. He bound the wound himself with a forge rag and kept working until the battle ended.

Arnwick fell anyway. He walked to Hearthmoor carrying one of the apprentices who hadn't made it out on their own. He's been here since.

His prosthetic is his own work — iron and hardwood, articulated at two points, fitted with a custom grip for smithing tools. He has made six versions of it over the decades. The current one is the best. He will talk at length about the engineering if you let him.

The arm he lost is not why he doesn't talk much about Ember Road. The thing he doesn't talk about is what they were actually defending: not a supply line. The supply line was a cover story for a convoy transporting something. He was told it was a weapon. He now believes it was a Shard. He does not know which one, does not know who ordered the convoy, does not know where it ended up.

He's been building a replica Crown for eleven years. It doesn't work. He knows it doesn't work. He keeps building it because solving the problem of why it doesn't work is the thing that will tell him what a Crown that does work actually is.

*What he needs from you:* Boss materials. Not for the economy of it — he has a theory that certain materials have residual dimensional resonance that the replica needs. Each time a significant boss is defeated, he'll ask for something specific from what they left behind. Each material advances the replica. Each advance reveals something about the original Crown's construction.

*What his questline is actually about:* By the third quest, the replica will briefly activate. It will produce a resonance pulse that is clearly perceived by something in the Void. The Voidlords will demonstrate awareness of Hearthmoor for the first time since the Sealing. Gareth will realize that he has not been building a replica. He has been building a signal.

## Mira

Age: forty-four.

She came to Hearthmoor as a traveling herbalist seven years ago, intending to stay a season and catalog the unusual plants that grow near the Void traces at the valley's edge. She stayed because the catalog kept expanding and because Elder Lyra offered her a workshop.

The plants near Void traces are not corrupted versions of normal plants. They are normal plants that have adapted to different conditions — grown slightly sideways from what they should be, evolved responses to a changed environment. Most of them are useless or toxic. A few of them are interesting. One of them, which she has named Nullwort for the moment she realized what it did, is a plant she has told no one about except Elder Lyra.

Nullwort, when prepared correctly, temporarily reduces a person's perception of Void influence. Not their vulnerability to it — their perception. Someone under Nullwort's effect can walk through a dense Void trace and feel nothing wrong. They can look at a Voidlord construct and see only an object in space.

She has not published this. She is not sure whether it is a medicine or a catastrophically dangerous thing. A person who cannot perceive Void influence is a person who cannot be warned by their own senses that something is going wrong. The protection is not real — only the perception changes. She has been sitting with this problem for three years.

She is also the only person in Hearthmoor who has a theory about what the Void corruption is actually doing to the soil. Her theory is that it is not corrupting — it is adapting. The soil near Void traces is not dying. It is slowly becoming capable of supporting Void-origin life. What the world looks like if that process completes is something she would rather not think about.

*What her questline is about:* The truth about Nullwort. Three quests: the discovery of what it actually does, the discovery that someone has been using it, and the discovery of who is growing it deliberately and what they intend. The answer is not a villain. It's someone making a reasonable but dangerous decision under terrible pressure, and Mira will ask you to help her figure out a better one.

## Dorin

Age: unknown. Appears late forties. Has appeared late forties for at least twelve years, according to people who have known him that long.

He runs the market stall. He is very good at acquiring things that are hard to acquire and has a gift for knowing what people will want before they know they want it. He is pleasant company, genuinely funny in a dry way, and deflects personal questions with such grace that most people don't notice they've been deflected.

He went through a Void Gate eleven years ago. He came back four days later. He will not explain what he saw, what he did, or why he returned. He will not explain how he survived. He will not explain what he brought back.

What is observable: he does not age. The twelve-year witnesses are reliable people. He sleeps, eats, and breathes normally, which means whatever changed isn't obviously supernatural in its mechanics. He is occasionally seen, in the early morning before the village is awake, standing at the valley edge looking in the direction of the old Concordat ruins with an expression that could be read as listening.

The thing he brought back from the Void Gate is in his stall. It is not for sale. It is not displayed. It is in a locked box under the counter that he has never opened in front of anyone. He refers to it occasionally as "the accounting." He declines to explain what this means.

*What his questline is about:* He will, after sufficient trust is built, show you the accounting. It is a ledger — or something that presents as a ledger — written in a language that does not exist, containing entries that appear to be transactions. Names on one side, quantities on the other. The names include Seraphine Vael. The quantities are not money. He will tell you, in a flat voice, what he understands it to mean, and then he will tell you why he came back and what he has been doing in Hearthmoor for eleven years.

He came back because the accounting named him, and someone needed to be here when it mattered.

## Captain Vel

Age: fifty-eight.

She has held defensive lines at four settlements before Hearthmoor. Arnwick. Greenwater Ford. The Bridgehead at Copperpass. The Mill at Sallow Ridge. She was at each one when it fell. She survived each time, which she describes as a failing rather than an achievement.

Her standing orders at Hearthmoor are, as far as anyone can tell, written by herself for herself: hold the valley entrance, maintain the watch rotation, do not retreat without her explicit order. No one issued these orders to her. She arrived nine years ago, assessed the defenses, wrote the standing orders, posted them on the guardhouse wall, and began enforcing them. Elder Lyra recognized what she was doing and has never interfered.

She knows exactly what fell the other four settlements. Not the same thing in each case. Arnwick was the Ember Road campaign's aftermath. Greenwater Ford was internal — a faction that decided to try bargaining with the Voidlords. Copperpass was simply overwhelmed by numbers. The Mill at Sallow Ridge was different, and she doesn't talk about what happened there.

Her attitude toward Hearthmoor is not hope. She is not capable of hope for settlements anymore. She has watched too many of them end. Her attitude is something more like professional obligation: she knows how to hold a line, Hearthmoor has a line that needs holding, and leaving would be a kind of desertion. Not because anyone requires her to stay. Because she requires herself to.

*What her questline is about:* The Battle of Ember Road, and what she knows. By the third quest, she will tell you about the convoy — that it was a Shard, that she knew what it was, that she made a decision at the battle to let the line break rather than let the convoy be captured. The settlement fell because of her decision. The Shard made it out because of her decision. She has been living with both of these facts for thirty years.

What she needs is not absolution. She will tell you, clearly, that she would make the same decision again. What she needs is for you to understand why, and to decide whether she was right. She will listen to your answer. She will not be changed by it. But she needs to hear it from someone who has reason to judge.
