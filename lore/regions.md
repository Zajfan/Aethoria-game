# Regions of Aethoria

The world procedurally generates from seed 42 — every player shares the same terrain. What follows is the lore interpretation of what that terrain means: why each region is the way it is, what happened there, and what it holds now.

---

## Elandor Plains

**Threat level:** Low. Starting region.

Before the Shattering, the Plains were exactly what they look like: productive agricultural land, scattered villages, trade routes. The breadbasket of the old kingdoms. The landscape you see now is the same landscape — the farms, the paths, the villages — but with three hundred years of slow abandonment. Some farms are still worked by families who have been here since before the Shattering and have no particular interest in leaving. Most are overgrown.

The Void traces in the Plains soil are not dramatic. They are subtle: a field where seeds germinate sideways. A path where travelers occasionally lose thirty minutes without experiencing the passing. A well where the water tastes different at night. None of it is obviously dangerous. All of it is wrong in small, accumulating ways.

The corruption is worst in the northeast, near the old Concordat ruins. The ruins mark the point where the first Shard came down. The land around them has been too strange to farm for a hundred years.

The Void Knight emerged from the ruins within a decade of the Shattering. It does not hunt. It holds ground. The ruins are its territory, the Shard its charge. People who enter the ruins come back changed, or do not come back. The stories have been building for three centuries.

*What the Plains teach you:* That collapse is not an event but a process. That a world can end slowly, field by field, and people will adapt to each change because no single change is unbearable.

## Whispering Marshes

**Threat level:** Medium.

The Marshes were always liminal — a region between things, neither solid ground nor open water, neither day nor full dark because the fog diffuses the light. Pre-Shattering scholars considered them a reliable source of unusual alchemical components because the biology of transitional ecosystems produces organisms that don’t appear elsewhere.

After the Shattering, the Void trace that settled into the Marshes interacted with this liminality in unexpected ways. The spirits that travelers encounter are real — not hallucinations, not tricks of the fog. They are entities of some kind that the Marshes’ particular dimensional texture allows to persist. They are not hostile, exactly. They are indifferent in a way that resembles hostility because their goals and human goals do not overlap.

The scholars came to the Marshes. Many of them stayed — drawn by the forbidden knowledge available there, the texts and artifacts that couldn’t exist in a world where the boundary held but do exist in a world where it doesn’t. Some of them became the spirits. The line between scholar and spirit is something Mira is documenting and has not finished documenting.

The Sunken Vaults are the Marshes’ deepest point — a pre-Shattering facility that collapsed partially into the wetland after the Shattering destabilized the region. What was stored there is partially catalogued in a document that exists in three copies, two of which are in the Vaults themselves. The Lich King has been here since he chose undeath, maybe sixty years after the Shattering. He is not a villain in the simple sense. He is a very old thing with complicated reasons for being where he is.

*What the Marshes teach you:* That knowledge and safety are not the same thing. That some things are preserved for reasons that made sense to someone.

## Shattered Coast

**Threat level:** Medium-High.

The coastline was shattered — literally — by the seismic activity that accompanied the Shattering. The cliffs here are new, geologically speaking, formed in a matter of days three hundred years ago. The sea temples are much older. They predate the Concordat by centuries and were built by people who are not well-documented anywhere.

What is documented: the temples were built at specific points along the coast that correspond to nodes in the dimensional boundary. Whoever built them knew the boundary existed and was doing something with that knowledge. The current working theory, developed by the small community of scholars in Hearthmoor who study this, is that the temple builders were attempting their own version of what the Concordat later attempted: boundary management. They may have been doing it successfully for a long time before whatever ended them.

The Void Herald is not a Voidlord. It is something the Voidlords shaped from the boundary material around the third Shard’s resting place — a kind of local presence. It is not intelligent in the human sense. It is purposeful. It was made for one task and it is performing that task: holding the Shard’s location stable until the agreement is completed.

The pirates are entirely human and entirely pragmatic. They found that the coast’s strangeness kept organized authority away and moved in. They have developed a folk knowledge of which temples are safe to enter, which are not, and what the Herald will and will not respond to. Some of this knowledge is accurate.

*What the Coast teaches you:* That other people had this problem before and solved it in ways that weren’t preserved. That the absence of a record is not evidence of an absence.

## Ashveil Peaks

**Threat level:** High.

The Peaks are volcanic. They were volcanic before the Shattering — the dwarves who lived here built their culture around metalwork and heat management, mining ore from the volcanic geology. They are more volcanic now: the Shattering destabilized the tectonic plates underneath the range, and there has been persistent seismic activity for three centuries.

The dwarves are gone. The question of what happened to them is answered in fragments scattered through the ruins: some left for lower ground when the seismic activity became severe. Some stayed too long. Some encountered the thing in the lowest level of the deepest mine — not a Void trace but a Void intrusion, a place where the boundary had worn completely through in a small area — and made a decision. The decision produced the Stone Colossus.

The Stone Colossus was not built to be a guardian of the Shard. It was built to do something else: to be the last act of a community that knew it was ending and wanted to leave something behind. The dwarves gave it the instruction to protect what was most important in the deepest chamber. The most important thing in the deepest chamber, by the time they finished the Colossus, was the fourth Shard.

The Colossus has been doing exactly what it was told to do for two hundred and seventy years. It does not reason about the instruction. It does not question it. It is not malicious. It is thorough.

*What the Peaks teach you:* That protection and imprisonment are sometimes the same thing. That people who build lasting things should be careful about the instructions they give.

## Crystal Wastes

**Threat level:** Endgame. This region is not survivable without significant preparation.

The Crystal Wastes were not always frozen. Three hundred years ago this was temperate — high altitude, cold, but livable. The Shard that fell here changed the local climate. Not immediately, but progressively over decades, as the dimensional boundary degraded and the Void’s different physical laws began to bleed into the space around the fifth Shard’s resting place.

The Wastes are now a region where Aethoria’s physics and Void-adjacent physics overlap imperfectly. The ice crystals that cover the ground and the spires that rise from it are not made of water. They are made of something that looks like ice and behaves like ice in most respects but has different properties under specific conditions. Mira has a sealed jar of it. She keeps it in the cold room. She will not open the jar.

The Lich King is here. The same Lich King who is also in the Sunken Vaults — undeath, in Aethoria, is not a single location. He has been maintaining two presences since approximately the point he realized there were two Shards he needed to watch. The fifth Shard is not in a dungeon. It is on him, or in him, in a way that makes the distinction unclear.

The Crystal Wastes contain the highest concentration of Void influence in the accessible world. Enemies here are different: not just corrupted, but partially translated. They behave according to Void logic overlaid on Aethorian bodies. Status effects work differently. Aggro radiuses function differently. The rules you learned in the Plains do not fully apply here.

*What the Wastes teach you:* What the world becomes if the Crown is not restored. And also: the Lich King is not there because the Void trapped him. He is there by choice. The question of why is one of the things you will need to resolve before the ending makes sense.

## Hearthmoor Valley

**Not a combat region.** The hub.

The valley sits in a geographic configuration that creates a natural thickening of the dimensional boundary — the Sealing reinforced this, but the natural protection existed before Lyra’s working. It is not safe in absolute terms. Void traces reach the valley’s edge at dusk and occasionally cross it. But it is orders of magnitude safer than the surrounding regions, and this is why there are people here rather than somewhere else.

The village is not particularly large. It was never meant to become what it has become — a permanent settlement, the last functioning community for two days’ travel in most directions. It grew through accretion: a family that stopped passing through, a scholar who needed a base, a smith who heard there was stable work. Over three hundred years these accumulations became a place with its own character.

You arrived this morning. Nobody knows why you came here, including you. Elder Lyra looked at you for a long time before she spoke.

She has looked at people like that before.
