# Factions of Aethoria

The four factions that remain after the Shattering are not political parties or ideological movements. They are survival strategies. Each represents a different answer to the question of how to live in a world where the boundary between Aethoria and the Void is degrading.

Reputation with each faction runs from -1000 (openly hostile) to +1000 (full membership). Faction standing affects shop prices, quest availability, and NPC reactions. Some factions are natural rivals — high standing with one lowers standing with another.

---

## Hearthmoor

**Standing range:** Starts at +100 (home settlement). Can reach +1000 through story quests and NPC questlines.

The people of Hearthmoor are not a faction in any organized sense. They are the survivors of three hundred years of managed disaster. They have no ideology beyond the practical: keep the valley, feed people, deal with each problem as it comes. Elder Lyra has run this for centuries not because she believes in Hearthmoor's mission but because she believes in the competence of the people she has assembled here.

The faction system tracks your standing with Hearthmoor as a community. High standing means NPCs bring you information unprompted, market prices improve, and certain personal questlines open that do not open for strangers.

What Hearthmoor wants from you: results. Information about the Shards, materials that advance Gareth's research, safe passage for the supply routes that keep the village provisioned. Hearthmoor does not ask for loyalty. It asks for work done.

**Rival:** Voidborn (irreconcilable — Hearthmoor's purpose is to close the boundary; Voidborn's purpose is to open it further)

## The Guild

**Standing range:** Starts at 0. Requires completion of the first merchant quest to open.

The Guild is what remains of the pre-Shattering merchant class: the families and organizations that managed trade routes, banking, and information networks before the kingdoms fell. They survived the Shattering better than most because they had already distributed their operations across multiple settlements — when each settlement fell, the Guild lost a branch but not the network.

They are not good people in any morally uncomplicated sense. They trade in everything. Some of what they trade in should not be traded. Their primary concern is the continuation of commerce, which requires stable conditions, which means they have a pragmatic interest in slowing the Void expansion — but not necessarily in stopping it. A partially corrupted world with functioning trade routes is preferable, to the Guild, to a fully corrupted world. Whether that preference extends to supporting the Crown restoration depends on who is doing the asking and what they're offering.

What the Guild wants from you: profitable arrangements. They will give you access to better gear, rare materials, and information networks in exchange for jobs — some straightforward (escort a shipment), some less so (recover a debt from someone who survived by moving somewhere dangerous).

**Faction bonus:** +15% sell price on looted items. Access to rare item stock that isn't available on the open market.

**Rival:** The Order (philosophical — Order distrusts commerce; Guild distrusts piety)

## The Order

**Standing range:** Starts at 0. Requires completing the first bounty from Captain Vel or Elder Lyra.

The Order of the Sealed Gate is a military-religious organization that predates the Shattering by about a century — they were originally a small sect who believed that the Voidlords were a serious threat and that the Crystal Crown needed active protection rather than ceremonial display. Nobody listened to them before the Shattering. The Shattering made them correct.

They are not a church in the traditional sense. They do not worship a god. They have doctrine — specific practices and protocols for dealing with Void-adjacent threats — and they have hierarchy. Their current leadership structure descends directly from the pre-Shattering sect, although the gap between the founding and the present means that much of the original doctrine has been reinterpreted, sometimes usefully and sometimes not.

The Order believes the Crown must be restored. They believe this with the intensity of people who have been right about something terrible for three hundred years and watched everyone else refuse to believe them. This makes them capable and effective and also inflexible in ways that occasionally cause serious problems.

What they want from you: to be the thing they cannot be — a free agent. The Order has enemies and political complications that prevent them from acting directly in certain locations. You do not have those limitations. High Order standing opens access to advanced combat training, information about enemy movements, and equipment designed specifically for Void-adjacent combat.

**Faction bonus:** +15% attack vs. Void enemies. Access to Order-specific crafting recipes.

**Rival:** Guild (philosophical). Deep rivalry with Voidborn.

## Voidborn

**Standing range:** Starts at -200 (hostile). Can be brought to 0 through specific questlines and choices. Cannot reach positive standing without making choices that lower other faction standings significantly.

The Voidborn are not villains. This is important. They are people who have concluded, based on evidence available to them, that the Void boundary's restoration is not the correct response to the situation.

Their central argument: the boundary has been degrading for three hundred years. The world has adapted. Plants grow near Void traces. Animals evolve alongside them. Some people, exposed to Void influence over generations, perceive things differently — not worse, differently. The Voidborn believe that the correct response to the Shattering is not to close the door but to learn to live with it open. To study what the Voidlords actually are rather than fear them. To negotiate.

This position is not insane. The evidence they cite is real. Their representatives are, individually, often thoughtful people who have reached their conclusions through genuine consideration rather than corruption.

The Voidborn also contain, at their edges, people who have been in contact with the Voidlords long enough that it is not clear they are still making autonomous decisions. The faction's leadership is aware of this and considers it an acceptable cost. The faction's leadership may also be subject to the same process and lack the perspective to recognize it.

What they want from you: to see things their way. Or, failing that, to not actively work against them. They have information about the Shards — they have been studying the Crown problem from their own angle for decades. Some of that information is not available anywhere else.

**Faction bonus:** Access to unique Void-origin item crafting. Resistance to Void status effects at high standing.

**Cost of high standing:** Order and Hearthmoor standings decrease. Some NPCs stop talking to you.
